### Decisions that bind

- **Tools are judged against each other** (BP3). **But a slider's math is MaterialX's** (lead, 2026-09-24, LCDSchema: *"the number we see in [a consuming application] is the number we see in a stock USD viewer"*). So the Blender masters copy the MaterialX math (UV scale divides, rotation turns counter-clockwise about the UV origin, offset is subtracted after rotation; roughness bias is added, then clamped to 0–1), and the rig measures how close the *pictures* come.
- **The formulas are `MasterSet.md`'s:** the overlay mix (it bends the normal and roughens; it never tints), mask channels (R = layer 2; G, B, A gate overlays 1–3), the two-layer blend, layer scale (Phase04), and the opacity floor of 0.05 on the see-through masters. Overlays and masks load as data (linear), never as colour.
- **Each master's settings** (cut-out, two-sided, refraction, subsurface) follow `MasterSet.md`'s settings table.
- **Bars:** colour difference under 2 is the target for Opaque, Masked and Emissive, and "recognisable" for see-through and subsurface (Decision of record 9). **The first numbers set the final bar per master;** the target is not assumed.
- **Blender's parity render is Cycles** (the faithful one; Phase06 can pair it with Unreal's path tracer).
- **The Blender look-alike is replaced** (lead, 2026-09-26, verbatim: *"Yes - the goal indeed is to get things actaully working so we see how it is in practice"*). This retires the 2026-07 directive "recognizable, not faithful" for creators' Blender library. The replacement keeps the exported slider sockets, so the Carrier rule output is unchanged.
- **Nothing in `library/releases/` is touched** (research Pass 3 item 4).

### Known gaps to expect (from the docs; the rig confirms or clears each)

- **USDLiveView's renderer shows see-through as transparency, not refraction** (Pass 2): what is behind shows through, straight and unbent. Glass is judged as "recognisable" (clear or tinted, how see-through) in both columns, and fully once Unreal arrives. **Two measured defects to chase in 5.3:** green glass reads far too dark, and clear glass shows a speckled highlight that opaque materials don't.
- **Blender's Principled BSDF likely has no separate transmission colour and no thin-walled mode** (unverified). Glass tint may need handling in the Blender master.
- **Emission units differ** (MaterialX luminance vs Blender strength). Neon calibrates on this.
- **Rasteriser vs path tracer:** glossy highlights will differ a little by nature; that is what per-master bars are for.

### Pass 2 (2026-09-26): can USDLiveView's renderer show glass? (lead question)

**Examined:** Storm's MaterialX handling in the local USD source (`pxr/imaging/hdSt/materialXFilter.cpp`) and `~/usd-tools/apply-openpbr-patch.sh`. **Probe:** Glass_Clear and Glass_Green rendered with `usdrecord`, with and without a UV-grid wall behind them (`/tmp/p05probe`).

**Findings:**
- **F6 — Storm shows see-through by transparency, not refraction.** An OpenPBR material with `transmission_weight > 0` is tagged "translucent", and Storm sets MaterialX's *opacity* method for transmission (the source says so, to keep pre-1.38.5 behaviour). What is behind shows through, unbent.
- **F7 — "Renders black" was the empty background.** In front of the grid, clear glass is a clear veil and green glass tints the grid. The skill's §6 note is stale (→ corrected in this phase).

- **F9 — Real bending needs a different renderer**, not a setting. Storm's other MaterialX mode (refraction) bends only the sky map, not objects in the scene. A true refractive look in USDLiveView means a path-tracing renderer inside USDLiveView: its own project's work. We already patch our local USD build once (`apply-openpbr-patch.sh`), so a small Storm fix for F8 is possible, but it helps only builds that carry the patch.
