**Four things to look at first:**
- **On a coated article, the wear layers and the roughness slider cannot reach the gloss.** Paint_Gloss carries its gloss in the OpenPBR coat (`coat_roughness` 0.05). The assembler writes `coat_roughness` as a plain constant. Overlays and `roughness_bias` change only the body's roughness under the coat. So scratches and fingerprints change nothing in any tool, and roughness ±0.5 barely moves (Unreal 1–2.5, the others under 0.7). A scratched gloss paint cannot be shown. This is a library limit, not the recipe. It is not one of the known findings (F-P11-9 is about rough matter).
- **A Subsurface article at `subsurface_weight` 1 cannot be tinted.** On Silicone_Translucent the tint changes nothing in any tool. At weight 1 the base colour is unused, and the assembler wires `subsurface_color` to the tinted base colour only for Hair and `base_color_map` articles. Every other Subsurface article states a constant scatter colour that the tint never reaches. Onyx (0.5) and Jade (0.6) still move with the tint, because part of their colour is still the base colour. I kept weight 1, which is right for uncoloured silicone, and did not trade the physics for a working slider. Your call.
- **PaintChip01 reads as chipped paint at the close-up but as a speckle at the wide view.** See Paint_OnMetal_Chipped below.
- **Paint_OnMetal_Chipped reuses MildSteel_Raw's steel textures** (Metal002) as its layer 2, from `engineered/metal`. If MildSteel_Raw is redone with another scan, this article changes with it. CREDITS.md's Metal002 row names only MildSteel_Raw. I did not add this article to it: that file is written by the importer, and an edit here would have needed a hand-edited row.
